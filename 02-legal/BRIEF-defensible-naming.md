# Fly GACA — Defensible Naming & Authority Brief

**Status:** decision input, not a decision · **Maps to:** `PHASE0.md` P0-2, `office/LAWYER-BRIEF.md`,
`ROADMAP.md` Phase 0 (name lock) · **Prepared:** 2026-05-30
**Companion:** `STRATEGY-competitive-teardown.md`

The competitive teardown names the brand as our single largest existential risk: the entire
identity leans on the regulator's name, and our own research (`PHASE0.md` P0-2) says it is
likely unregistrable and carries passing-off exposure. This brief frames the decision so it
can be made *with* the lawyer rather than drifting — and pairs it with the move that turns the
weakness around: **acquiring authority we cannot claim by name.**

> Not legal advice. The trademark verdict and the redistribution view are the Saudi IP
> lawyer's to give (`office/LAWYER-BRIEF.md`). This brief organises the options and the
> consequences; it does not decide them.

---

## 1. Why this gates everything

The name is on the critical path, not beside it. Per `ROADMAP.md`:

```
lawyer opinion → name lock → entity (P0-3) → business bank account → payments + VAT (Phase 5)
                       └→ final branding everywhere (repo, domains, assets)
```

Every downstream gate — entity, bank, payments, the `.sa` domain — waits on a locked name. A
late rebrand touches the repo, both domains, every brand asset, and the registered entity
name. It is **far cheaper to absorb before the public launch than after.** Treating the name as
a parallel "someday" track is the mistake; it is *the* track.

## 2. The risk, restated plainly

- **"GACA" is the well-known designation of a Saudi government authority.** Saudi trademark law
  bars marks that consist of names specific to the Kingdom or official marks "unless authorised
  by the entity that owns them," and SAIP can refuse marks with misleading components or that
  imply official endorsement.
- **A disclaimer is necessary but not sufficient** — it reduces confusion but does not cure use
  that is misleading overall.
- **Competitor angle:** our name is a lever a rival can pull — by signing the authority we can't
  claim, or by drawing a regulator's attention to a name that leans on theirs. We should not
  leave that lever lying on the table.

## 3. The three paths (for the lawyer to weigh)

| Path | What it is | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Keep "Fly GACA"** | Accept it may be unregistrable; mitigate with prominent disclaimers everywhere | Zero rebrand cost; existing equity | Unregistrable mark; ongoing passing-off exposure; the lever stays in a rival's hand |
| **B — Demote to tagline** | Register a *different* primary mark; "for GACA / Saudi aviation" becomes descriptive copy | Protects a real mark; keeps discoverability | Some rebrand cost; "Fly GACA" descriptive use still needs care |
| **C — Rebrand the primary name** | A defensible mark with no "GACA" in it; pure descriptive reference to GACAR only where factual | Cleanest legal posture; a registrable, ownable asset | Highest switching cost; do it *before* launch |

**"Captain Adel" is unaffected in all three** — it is already a defensible, ownable brand and
`captadel.com` is secured. That matters: it is a ready-made primary or umbrella brand if the
verdict pushes toward B or C.

## 4. Naming criteria (if B or C)

A candidate primary name should be:
- **Registrable at SAIP** — no Kingdom-specific or authority designations; no implication of
  official endorsement.
- **Defensible / ownable** — distinctive enough to protect, not generic.
- **Bilingual-clean** — works in EN and AR, no awkward transliteration; brand wordmark stays LTR.
- **Domain + handle available** — `.com` at minimum; `.sa` revisited after the entity exists.
- **Descriptive-reference-safe** — we can still say "Saudi civil-aviation regulations / GACAR"
  as factual description without it being the *mark*.

`Captain Adel` is the strongest asset already in hand and should anchor the shortlist.

## 5. The authority move (turning the weakness around)

Because we can never *claim* official status, the durable answer is to **acquire borrowed
authority through relationships** — the thing a name can't buy and a competitor would race for:

- An **ATO / flight-academy partnership** ("the prep platform used by [Academy]") — see
  `GTM-schools.md`. This is the highest-leverage authority signal and doubles as the B2B revenue
  channel.
- **Optional written clarification from GACA's Data Management Office** (`boad@gaca.gov.sa`,
  noted in `PHASE0.md` P0-1) — even a neutral acknowledgement reduces ambiguity.
- **Credible independent positioning** — accuracy, citations, and the freshness guarantee
  (`SPEC-freshness-pipeline.md`) as the trust substitute for an official badge we cannot wear.

The strategic point: a defensible *name* removes a legal liability; a partnership *relationship*
supplies the authority the market actually wants. Do both — and do the name first, because it
gates the entity that lets the partnership be contracted and invoiced.
