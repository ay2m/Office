## Why This Guide Exists / لماذا هذا الدليل

Until now, Fly GACA's curriculum materials were built for academies and instructors. This guide is built for **you** — the individual who wants to earn a Saudi PPL (or progress toward a CPL or ATPL) and is doing a significant part of the theoretical groundwork on their own. It shows you how the five parts of Fly GACA fit together as a personal study system, and gives you a phase-by-phase plan that takes you from first login to mock-exam readiness.

## Part 1 — Understanding Your Five Study Tools
### الجزء الأول — أدواتك الخمس للدراسة

Fly GACA is built around five integrated sections. Learning how they connect is itself the first study skill.

---

### 1. Library / المكتبة
**What it is:** The authoritative reference layer. The Library hosts the GACARs (the Saudi civil aviation regulations), ICAO standards, approved handbooks, aeronautical information publications, and other primary documents.

**How to use it in self-study:**
- Always go here **to verify** — not to read from scratch.
- When Captain Adel teaches a rule (e.g. VFR minima), open the corresponding GACAR in the Library and read the actual text. Adel explains; the Library is the source of truth.
- Bookmark the GACAR Parts you use most: Part 61 (Licensing), Part 91 (General Operating Rules), Part 1 (Definitions).
- Use the Library's search to look up any term Adel mentions that you are not certain about.

**When NOT to rely on it alone:** The Library presents documents as published at a point in time. Check GACA's official website for the most current version before any licensing decision.

---

### 2. Captain Adel / الكابتن عادل
**What it is:** Fly GACA's AI ground-school tutor — a conversational instructor trained on Saudi aviation context and PPL ground-school subjects. Adel teaches concepts, explains regulations in plain language, answers follow-up questions, and works through practice problems with you.

**How to use Captain Adel effectively:**

*Start every topic with the prompt the Ground School module gives you.* Each lesson in Ground School includes a ready-made "Adel prompt" — a carefully phrased question that opens the topic at exactly the right level. Use it. Then ask follow-up questions freely.

*Probe and challenge.* After Adel explains something, ask: "Can you give me a Saudi-specific example?" or "What would a PPL exam question look like on this topic?" or "Where does GACAR specifically address this?"

*Use Adel for calculation practice.* Weight-and-balance problems, E6B problems, fuel calculations — Adel can walk you through worked examples. Do the arithmetic yourself first, then compare.

*Use Adel for mnemonics and pattern recognition.* Ask: "Give me a memory aid for the airspace VMC minima table."

**The limits of Captain Adel — please read:**
- Adel is an AI. He can make errors, reflect outdated information, or phrase a regulatory statement imprecisely.
- **Never rely on an Adel answer about a regulation without checking the Library and the actual GACAR.**
- Adel is not a flight instructor and does not replace your real instructor's briefings.
- For anything operational — actual flight planning, airspace clearances, NOTAMs, weather decisions — use official sources only.
- If Adel and the Library disagree, the Library (and ultimately GACA) is correct.

---

### 3. Flight Tools / أدوات الطيران
**What it is:** Interactive calculators and trainers — the E6B flight computer, weight-and-balance tool, airspace structure viewer, VFR quick card, and chart-symbols trainer.

**How to use it in self-study:**
- Use the Tools **after** a lesson, not instead of it. First understand the concept with Adel and the Library, then practise the calculation with the Tool.
- The E6B tool is especially valuable: work through a wind-triangle problem by hand, then verify with the tool. Repeat until the method is automatic.
- The Airspace tool reinforces the airspace lesson visually — spend time clicking through Saudi FIR structure.
- The VFR quick card is a study aid, not an in-flight checklist. Use it to memorise the VMC minima table.
- The Weight & Balance tool lets you vary the loading scenario — try loading the aircraft outside the envelope deliberately to see the effect.

---

### 4. Guides / الأدلة
**What they are:** Plain-language orientation articles covering the Saudi licensing journey — PPL requirements, the medical process, English language proficiency, the skills test, and progression routes.

**How to use it in self-study:**
- Read the Guides **first**, before starting Ground School, to understand the overall journey you are on.
- The Guide on Saudi PPL requirements gives you the framework (age, medical class, flight hours, theoretical knowledge exams, skills test) so you know what each study phase is building toward. **The Guide is for orientation — always verify current numbers with GACA and your FTO.**
- The medical Guide helps you understand the Class 2 medical before you book an appointment — but does not replace your Aviation Medical Examiner (AME).
- Return to the Guides when you have questions about the process (e.g. "can I log hours in a non-Saudi-registered aircraft?") — and then verify with your FTO.

---

### 5. Ground School / المدرسة الأرضية
**What it is:** The structured theoretical knowledge programme — nine subjects, 34 lessons, each with learning objectives, a Captain Adel teaching prompt, a linked reading resource, and a quiz bank. This is the core of your exam preparation.

**How to use it in self-study:**
- Follow the module sequence below. The ordering is deliberate.
- Complete each lesson: read the objective → use the Adel prompt → read the linked resource in the Library → take the module quiz.
- Do not skip to the quiz. The quiz tests what the lesson teaches; without the lesson, you are just memorising answers.
- After completing all nine modules, use the full 14-bank quiz shuffle and the PPL Mock Exam (Set A) as your readiness assessment.

## Part 2 — Your Licensing Journey Context
### الجزء الثاني — سياق رحلة الحصول على الرخصة

> This section is orientation only. Requirements are set by GACA and your FTO. Read the **Guides** section of Fly GACA for more detail, then verify everything with your FTO and directly with GACA before making decisions.

A Saudi PPL requires you to satisfy several parallel tracks simultaneously — theoretical knowledge is only one of them. Understanding all tracks from the start helps you plan your time.

| Track | What it involves | Where to learn more |
|---|---|---|
| Medical | Class 2 medical certificate from a GACA-approved AME | Fly GACA Guides → Medical |
| English Language | ICAO language proficiency (Level 4 minimum) | Fly GACA Guides → English Proficiency |
| Flight Hours | Minimum flight hours including solo, cross-country, and instrument time | Fly GACA Guides → PPL Requirements; GACAR Part 61 in the Library |
| Theoretical Knowledge | Written examination(s) covering the nine ground-school subjects | This guide + Ground School |
| Skills Test | Practical flight test with a GACA examiner | Your FTO |

**Recommendation:** Get your Class 2 medical early — ideally before you invest heavily in ground school — so that any medical factor is identified before you commit significant time and money. This is orientation advice, not medical advice; consult your AME and FTO.

## Part 3 — The Study Plan
### الجزء الثالث — خطة الدراسة

### Overview / نظرة عامة

The plan is structured in **four phases** totalling approximately **12 weeks** at a pace of 8–10 hours of self-study per week. If you can study more, compress it. If you are studying alongside full-time work or flight training, extend it. The phases are not calendar-locked; they are competency-locked — move to the next phase when you meet the milestone, not on a fixed date.

```
Phase 0 — Orientation       (1 week)
Phase 1 — Foundations       (Weeks 2–5, 4 modules)
Phase 2 — Operations        (Weeks 6–9, 4 modules)
Phase 3 — Integration       (Weeks 10–12, final module + consolidation)
Phase 4 — Mock Exam & Go    (Final readiness check — not time-bound)
```

#### Module 2 — Principles of Flight (Week 3)
**مبادئ الطيران**

| Lesson | Focus |
|---|---|
| POF-1: The four forces and lift | Angle of attack, Bernoulli, Newton |
| POF-2: Drag and the power curve | Induced vs parasite drag, best speeds |
| POF-3: Stability and control | CG, longitudinal/lateral/directional stability |
| POF-4: Stalls, spins and angle of attack | Stall as an AoA event, not a speed event |

**Weekly rhythm:** Follow the same 7-day pattern as Week 2.

**Key concepts to own:**
- Lift equation (qualitative, not just the formula)
- Why Vx ≠ Vy and when you use each
- What moves the stall speed (load factor, bank angle, weight)
- Why the stall is always an angle-of-attack event

**Captain Adel tip:** Ask Adel to walk you through a scenario: *"The aircraft is in a 60° banked level turn. What is the load factor, and how does the stall speed change?"*

#### Module 3 — Aircraft General Knowledge (Week 4)
**المعرفة العامة بالطائرة**

| Lesson | Focus |

| AGK-1: Airframe and flight controls | Structure, primary and secondary controls |

| AGK-4: Flight instruments | The six-pack, how each works, failure modes |

**Key concepts to own:**
- Carburettor ice conditions and the response
- What the vacuum system powers and what fails if it fails

- Instrument errors (e.g. altimeter lag, gyroscopic precession)

**Captain Adel tip:** For instruments, ask Adel: *"Walk me through what happens to each of the six instruments if I enter cloud and the vacuum pump fails."* This is a classic exam scenario.

#### Module 4 — Meteorology (Week 5)
**الأرصاد الجوية**

| Lesson | Focus |
|---|---|
| MET-1: The atmosphere and the ISA | Layers, lapse rates, ISA deviations |
| MET-2: Pressure, wind and altimetry | Pressure systems, QNH/QFE/Standard, transition alt |
| MET-3: Cloud, visibility and hazards | Thunderstorms, icing, turbulence, wind shear |
| MET-4: Reading METAR and TAF | Decode field by field using Saudi examples |

**Weekly rhythm:** Same 7-day pattern. For MET-4, spend extra time with real METARs — search for OEJN (Jeddah) or OERK (Riyadh) METARs online and decode them alongside the lesson.

**Key concepts to own:**
- ISA temperature at sea level and lapse rate
- QNH vs QFE vs standard setting — when to use which
- Conditions that produce carburettor icing (not just clouds — clear-air icing is possible)
- How to identify a SIGMET-worthy hazard in a TAF

**Captain Adel tip:** Paste a real Saudi METAR into your Adel conversation and ask him to decode it field by field.

**Phase 1 Milestone Check:**
Before moving to Phase 2, take the quiz banks for all four Phase 1 modules. Target: 75% or above on each bank before proceeding. If any bank is below 75%, spend an extra day on that subject before continuing.

#### Module 8 — Operational Procedures (Week 9)
**الإجراءات التشغيلية**

> Note: This is one of the recently added quiz banks. Lesson coverage may be expanding — check Ground School for the current lesson list.

**Integration review task (Week 10):**
Take all 14 quiz banks back to back in a single sitting — uninterrupted, timed, as if it were an exam. Do not look anything up. Record your score on each bank and compute a total. This gives you a baseline for Phase 4.

#### Weeks 11–12 — Weak-Subject Consolidation
**تعزيز المواد الضعيفة**

Take the results from your Week 10 integration quiz and rank your nine subjects from weakest to strongest. Spend Weeks 11–12 as follows:

- **Lowest-scoring subject:** Two full study days (Adel → Library → tools → quiz)
- **Second-lowest:** One full study day
- **Third-lowest:** One half-day
- **Everything else:** One revision session of key facts only

Do a second full integration quiz at the end of Week 12. Your target before entering Phase 4: **no subject below 70%, overall average 80%+.**

**Spaced-repetition with the quiz banks:**
The quiz banks are your spaced-repetition engine. Use them as follows:
- First attempt: no prep, diagnostic only.
- Review every question you got wrong: understand why (Adel → Library).
- Second attempt: 24 hours later.
- Third attempt: 1 week later.
- Fourth attempt: 2 weeks later.
This simple schedule leverages the testing effect — retrieval practice is more effective than re-reading.

### Phase 4 — Mock Exam & Go Decision
### المرحلة الخامسة: الاختبار التجريبي وقرار المضي قدمًا
#### When you are ready (not before Week 12)

**PPL Mock Exam — Set A**

The PPL Mock Exam (Set A) is available in Fly GACA. It is a full-length simulated written examination drawing across all nine subjects. Treat it exactly as a real exam:

- Block out the full time allowance — no interruptions.
- No notes, no Library, no Adel during the exam.
- Work through every question; if unsure, mark and return.
- Submit and review your results by subject.

**Readiness analytics:**
After the mock exam, Fly GACA's analytics show you your score by subject. Use this table to interpret:

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 90%+ | Strong — ready to consider booking | Verify booking process with your FTO |
| 80–89% | Good — minor consolidation needed | One more week on sub-80% subjects |
| 70–79% | Acceptable — more work needed | Return to Weeks 11–12 pattern |
| Below 70% | Not ready | Restart Phase 3 consolidation |

**Before you book a real exam:**
- Your FTO must confirm you are ready — they know the current exam format, pass mark, and any changes since this guide was written.
- Confirm the current theoretical knowledge examination syllabus with GACA. Exam content can change; this guide cannot track those changes.
- Ensure your medical, English proficiency, and flight-hour requirements are also on track — a pass in the theoretical exam is only one component.

## Part 4 — Study Techniques for Solo Learners
### الجزء الرابع — تقنيات الدراسة للطلاب المستقلين

### The Core Loop / الحلقة الأساسية

For every lesson, follow this loop:

```
1. Read the objective (Ground School)
2. Study with Adel (Captain Adel prompt)
3. Verify with the source (Library — relevant GACAR / handbook)
4. Practise with Tools (if a tool applies)
5. Test yourself (quiz bank)
6. Review wrong answers with Adel + Library
```

Never skip steps 3 or 6. These are the steps most self-studiers skip, and they are the steps that convert short-term familiarity into long-term understanding.

---

### Weekly Structure / البنية الأسبوعية

A productive study week for a solo PPL student looks like this:

| Day | Activity | Platform section |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | New lesson × 1 (objective + Adel + Library) | Ground School + Captain Adel + Library |
| Tuesday | New lesson × 1 (objective + Adel + Library) | Ground School + Captain Adel + Library |
| Wednesday | Tool practice for the week's subject | Flight Tools |
| Thursday | New lesson × 1 | Ground School + Captain Adel + Library |
| Friday | Quiz bank — current module, first attempt | Ground School quiz |
| Saturday | Review wrong answers; quiz bank second attempt | Captain Adel + Library + quiz |
| Sunday | Rest or light review (re-read one lesson objective + key facts) | Ground School |

Adjust for your own schedule, but try to keep at least one rest day and always do the review-wrong-answers step before your next lesson.

---

### Working with Captain Adel — Prompt Patterns
### نماذج للتفاعل مع الكابتن عادل

Beyond the built-in lesson prompts, these patterns work well:

**The Exam Question Generator:**
> "Give me five PPL written-exam style questions on [topic]. Don't give me the answers yet."
(Attempt them, then ask for answers and explanations.)

**The Scenario Walk-Through:**
> "Walk me through this scenario step by step: [describe a situation]. What should the pilot do at each decision point, and which regulation or principle applies?"

**The Comparison:**
> "What is the difference between [concept A] and [concept B]? Where does the confusion usually come from?"

**The Memory Aid:**
> "Give me a mnemonic or memory framework for [list or table]."

**The GACAR Pointer:**
> "Which GACAR Part and section would a student look at to verify what you just told me about [topic]?"
(Always use this last pattern when Adel states a regulation. Then go to the Library and read it.)

---

### Bilingual Study / الدراسة ثنائية اللغة

Fly GACA is designed to support Arabic-speaking students. Practical suggestions:

- If a concept is clearer in Arabic, ask Adel to explain it in Arabic. The same prompt, in Arabic, can unlock better comprehension.
- For technical terms, learn both the English and Arabic forms — your practical flying will use English phraseology, but written exam questions may appear in either language depending on your exam authority.
- The GACAR regulations are the authoritative Arabic text — some definitions read more precisely in Arabic than in English translation. If you are uncertain about a definition, compare both versions in the Library.

---

### Managing Motivation Over 12 Weeks / إدارة الدافعية على مدى 12 أسبوعًا

Solo self-study is hard because there is no classroom accountability. Practical approaches:

**Set a booking date as a target.** Talk to your FTO early; agree a rough target date for your theoretical knowledge exam. A real deadline changes how you study.

**Log your quiz scores.** Keep a simple spreadsheet or notebook: date, module, score. Watching the numbers climb is motivating. Watching a flat score tells you where to spend more time.

**Find a study partner.** Even one other student preparing for the same exam — even remotely — provides accountability, someone to quiz you, and someone to share good Adel prompts with.

**Celebrate phase milestones.** Finishing Phase 1 is real progress. Phase 2 completion means you have covered the bulk of the syllabus. Acknowledge these moments.

**When you get stuck:** Do not stay stuck alone. Ask Adel for a different explanation. Look in the Library for a different angle. If a concept genuinely won't click after three approaches, ask your FTO instructor — that is a good use of instructor time.

## Closing — Before You Rely on Anything Operationally
### الخاتمة — قبل الاعتماد على أي شيء في العمليات الفعلية

Fly GACA has helped you build knowledge. But knowledge from a study platform — any study platform — is preparation for decisions, not the basis for decisions.

**Before your real written exam:**
- Confirm the current syllabus, pass mark, format, and permitted materials with your FTO and with GACA directly.
- Ensure your mock exam results reflect genuine understanding, not familiarity with the question patterns.
- Check that all other PPL requirements (medical, hours, English proficiency) are progressing on schedule.

**Before you rely on anything operationally:**
- Verify every regulation you recall studying against the current GACAR text in the Library or — better — on the GACA official website.
- Obtain current weather from official SADIS/ATIS/MET sources, not from memory of a textbook explanation.
- File flight plans, obtain clearances, and check NOTAMs through official channels.
- Your flight instructor, your FTO, and GACA are your authorities. This platform is your study preparation.

**You are building toward something real.** The Saudi PPL is a serious qualification that will take you to real skies over real terrain. The knowledge you are building in ground school is the foundation of decision-making you will use as a pilot for the rest of your flying life. Study it seriously, verify it rigorously, and fly it safely.

حظًا موفقًا — Good luck. The skies are worth it.
